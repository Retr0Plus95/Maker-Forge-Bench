# Maker Forge Bench 0.3 "Board view" Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Bench shows, for every part, **what it connects to**, **which holes it goes in**, and **the
build step it belongs to, with why**, then draws the board in 3D with the schematic behind it.

**Order:** the owner is building the fume extractor now, on their own placement rather than exactly
sheet 4. So what helps whatever the layout comes first:
1. connections (true for any layout);
2. build steps and their reasons;
3. the 3D board, which shows sheet 4's placement until the owner's own placement is put into
   `board.py`.

**Architecture:**
- In the fume repo, `export_board.py` writes the project file's `board` and `build` sections from
  `board.py` and `build_steps.py`. `check.sh` runs it, so the file can never drift from the layout.
- In Bench, `project.js` reads and checks those sections, and the card, the steps panel and the 3D
  view use them.
- The 3D view uses three.js r128 as ES modules, self-hosted, the same version Maker Forge uses.

**Tech Stack:** As in 0.2. The fume repo uses Python 3.10+ standard library and its own `check.sh`.

**Spec:** `docs/design/2026-11-12-board-view-parts-ui-design.md`: §2 (what the fume repo gets),
§3 (the board section), §5 (the board view), §6a (undo and save), §8 (security) and §10.

## Global Constraints

- Everything in the 0.1 and 0.2 Global Constraints still holds: strict CSP, no `innerHTML`,
  `textContent` only for project text, undo for changes, `bench.`-prefixed saved data, plain words,
  LF, the commit trailer, and `check.sh` passing in both repos.
- **The board and build sections are untrusted.** They're checked before use:
  - holes are integer or half-integer numbers from 0.5 to 200;
  - at most 2,000 parts and 100 pins a part, 5,000 links of at most 50 points each, and 500 nets of
    at most 500 members each;
  - names and labels are at most 40 characters, and step text at most 2,000.
- **Fume repo rules:** layout changes go through `board.py`, `pcb.py` must pass, `test_checker.py`
  stays at 8/8, every builder-visible change gets a `CHANGELOG.md` line, and Claude commits and
  pushes once `check.sh` passes.
- **Hole coordinates are (column, row) from 1 at the top left, as in `board.py`.** The UI always
  says "sheet 4 position" for them, because the owner may place parts their own way.

## Review Focus

1. **A part with no board data** (the panel parts RV1, D2, D3, F1, SW1): the card shows its
   connections if it has any, and says "Not on the board". Tested in Task 2.
2. **A net with many members** (`GND` has 27): the card shows the first 8 and "and 19 more"; it
   doesn't flood the panel. Tested in Task 2.
3. **Half-hole bends in link paths** (`[30.5, 1.6]`): they're accepted, kept, and drawn. Tested in
   Tasks 1 and 4.
4. **A build step naming a part the project doesn't have:** it's shown, but marked as not found.
   Tested in Task 3.
5. **No WebGL** (some phones and remote desktops): the Board tab says so and points to sheet 4.
   Tested in Task 4.

### Task 1 (fume repo): Export the board and the build steps into `forge-project.json`

**Files** (in `G:\Solder Fume Extractor\Solder-Fume-Extractor`):
- Create: `hardware/layout/export_board.py`, `hardware/layout/build_steps.py`,
  `hardware/layout/test_export.py`
- Modify: `check.sh` (run the export, then its test), `forge-project.json` (generated `board` and
  `build`), `CHANGELOG.md`

**Interfaces:**
- `build_steps.py` defines `STEPS`, a list of `{ "id", "title", "why", "parts": [refs], "what": [...] }`
  in guide §13's order:
  1. bare-wire buses;
  2. insulated links;
  3. the two bridges;
  4. the DIP socket (notch LEFT);
  5. the power strip (J0, D1, C1A, C1B, U1, C2, C3, C10, J2, J3);
  6. the chip's support parts (R4, C6, C7);
  7. the fan drive and tach (R8, R12, R9, R13, R7, R5, Q1, R6);
  8. the panel side (R2, R3, C4, J5, J4, J6);
  9. the underside parts (C5, C8, Y1, C9);
  10. the power-up checks before the chip.

  `what` is any of `"buses" | "links" | "bridges"`. Each `why` is plain words from the guide (for
  example "Buses go in first while the board is empty: the wire lies flat and every hole is easy to
  reach").
- `export_board.py`:
  - `board_section(B) -> dict` gives
    `{"format": "perfboard", "source": "hardware/layout/board.py", "pitch": 2.54, "cols", "rows", "hole": 1.0, "parts", "buses", "links", "bridges", "nets", "netLabels", "spare"}`.
    - Each part is `{"type", "pins": {name: [c, r]}, "side": "bottom" if type == "underside" else "top"}`,
      plus `body`, `D`, `keepout`, `round` and `half` when `board.py` has them.
    - Buses are `{"row", "from", "to", "net", "label", "side": "bottom"}`, links are
      `{"path": [[c, r], ...], "side": "bottom"}` and bridges are `{"holes": [a, b], "side": "bottom"}`.
    - `netLabels` maps each bus net to its label (`P12V` → `+12 V`).
  - `build_section(steps, B) -> list` returns the steps, and **raises** if a step names a part
    `board.py` doesn't have, or if any board part is in no step or in two.
  - `main(path="forge-project.json")` reads the file, replaces only `board` and `build`, and writes
    it back with `ensure_ascii=False, indent=2` and LF.
- `test_export.py` (unittest):
  - every `board.py` part, pin, bus, link, bridge and net appears in the section;
  - half-hole points such as `[30.5, 1.6]` are kept;
  - every board part is in exactly one step;
  - `main` keeps `parts` and `sheets` byte-for-byte as values, and running it twice gives the same file.

- [ ] **Step 2:** Write `build_steps.py` and `export_board.py`. Run the tests. Expected: all pass.

### Task 3 (Bench): Build steps, with the why, one at a time

**Files:**
- Modify: `src/app.js`, `src/panel.js` (`renderSteps`), `src/index.html` (a `#steps` section in the
  panel), `src/style.css`
- Create: `tests/browser/steps.test.js`

**Behaviour:**
- **The steps section** (`#steps`): each step is a row showing its number, title and progress
  (`3 of 8 soldered`) for its parts.
- **Opening a step** shows its why, its parts as buttons, and Next and Back. Space or `→` goes to
  the next step, and `←` to the previous one, when the focus isn't in a field.
- **The current step's parts** are ringed on the sheet on screen, in a step colour different from
  the Find ring (`#28c4ff`). Steps whose `what` includes buses, links or bridges say so in words,
  for example "Do the buses on rows 2, 4, 6, 17, 18 and 19, plus the 0 V spur on row 8."
- **Tick all in this step** ticks the step's parts as one undoable change.
- **Missing parts** (`missing`) are listed as `Not in this project: ZZ9`.
- The open step is remembered with the progress (saved), but opening a step isn't an undoable
  change: it's a view.

### Task 4 (Bench): The 3D board, with the schematic behind

**Files:**
- Create:
  - `src/vendor/three-r128/three.module.js` and `src/vendor/three-r128/OrbitControls.js`, copied
    unchanged from the npm package `three@0.128.0`, plus `src/vendor/three-r128/LICENSE` (MIT);
  - `src/board3d.js`, `tests/unit/board3d-geometry.test.js`, `tests/browser/board3d.test.js`.
- Modify: `dev.py` (serve `src/vendor/three-r128/*.js` only: one fixed subfolder, no other
  subfolders), `src/app.js`, `src/index.html`, `src/style.css`, `tests/python/test_dev.py`

**Interfaces:**
- `board3d.js` exports `partGeometry(part, pitch) -> { kind, x, y, z, w, d, h, radius?, axis? }[]`.
  This is pure maths, unit-tested: positions in mm from the board's top-left hole; sizes taken from
  `pcb.py`'s typical bodies (resistor 6.3 × 2.3 mm, cans by `D`, DIP from its pins, TO-92 4.8 ×
  3.8 mm), labelled "typical size".
- It also exports `createBoard3D(canvasHost, board, { onPick(ref) }) -> { show(), hide(), select(ref), setStep(refs), dispose() }`:
  - a WebGL renderer with a transparent background, OrbitControls, and a Raycaster for picking;
  - holes drawn as instanced meshes;
  - buses, links and bridges on the underside as tubes;
  - Flip turns the camera to the underside.
- **The Board tab:**
  - It sits after the sheet tabs, and shows only when the project has `board` data.
  - The schematic (the first schematic sheet) stays in `#stage`, dimmed to 35 %, with the 3D canvas
    over it.
  - Clicking a part selects it everywhere (card, rings, steps).
  - Without WebGL it shows `This browser can't draw 3D here. Sheet 4 shows the same layout flat.`
