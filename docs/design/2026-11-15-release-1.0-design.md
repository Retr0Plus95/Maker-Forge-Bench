# Bench 1.0 "Launch": design

Status: **draft, waiting for the owner's approval** (2026-11-15). Approved so far: the three features
taken from Maker Forge Shape (A, B and C below), added to 1.0 at the owner's word ("Yes, add to 1.0").
The launch work that was already on the roadmap is listed in §5 and gets its own detail before the plan.

## 1. Why

Shape (today's Maker Forge) has grown a lot of circuit-board work inside its project-box maker. Three
pieces of it help Bench now, without waiting for 2.0:

- **Wire lengths to cut.** Shape tells you how much of each wire colour to cut. Bench lists the links
  only as a count ("44 insulated links: see the wire list on sheet 4"), so the builder measures by hand.
- **Boards and modules.** Shape knows 63 boards and modules (Arduinos, Raspberry Pis, ESP32 boards,
  converters, chargers, relay modules, sensors) at their real size, with mounting holes and ports.
  Bench's library has 193 parts but only one module, the MP1584.
- **Send to Shape.** Shape already keeps "Your parts" (boards of your own) in the browser. Once Bench is
  live at forge.retr0plus.se/bench/ (1.0), both apps share one address and so one browser storage.
  Bench can then hand its board to Shape's box maker with no change to Shape. That brings the first
  piece of 2.0's "board to box" forward to 1.0, which is the release that puts Bench on that address.

Shape is the owner's own repo under MIT, and nothing here changes it. Code and data come over as
copies, rewritten to Bench's rules where needed (§6).

## 2. A. Wire lengths to cut

**What the builder gets:** Wiring tab → a new **Wire to cut** section.

- Totals first, in plain words: "Insulated wire: 44 links, about 1.3 m in all. Bare wire: 7 buses,
  about 0.7 m." The 1.3 m and 0.7 m are made-up examples.
- Then one row per link: where it runs (hole to hole, "24,10 → 28,12"), its line's name when it has
  one, and **cut 30 mm, strip 5 mm at each end**. Bare buses get "cut 85 mm" with no stripping.
- Bridges are solder blobs, so they get no row.
- The guide's links step changes from "see the wire list on sheet 4" to "44 insulated links, about
  1.3 m of wire: the lengths are in Wiring → Wire to cut".

**My board too:** the suggested wires on your own board (0.5's suggested wires, one group per line)
get the same lengths. Each line's total comes with a colour hint: red for supply lines, black for
0 V, any other colour for signals. Bench already sorts lines into those three kinds.

**The rule (my call, a builder may tune it):**

- The length along the path: each step between holes is its true distance × the pitch, so diagonals
  count as diagonals.
- Plus 5 mm at each end: through the board and bent over or soldered.
- Rounded up to the next 5 mm, which is easier to cut with a ruler.
- Shape's rule for box wires (plus 10 %, plus 20 mm) is for loose wires in a case. Perfboard links
  lie flat, so they get the tighter rule.

**Numbers:** links are numbered in the project's order. Check this against sheet 4's wire list on the
fume extractor. If the two orders differ, show hole to hole only, with no numbers, so a "Link 18"
never means two different wires.

## 3. B. Boards and modules from Shape, in the Maker Forge Library

**What the builder gets:** a project part whose value is "Arduino Nano", "ESP32 DevKit", "LM2596" and
so on gets:

- a 3D model at its real size, with its mounting holes and its ports (USB, DC jack, screw terminals)
  where they really are;
- a part card with the size, the holes and the ports;
- an entry in Looks like → 3D model, for every part of the module type.

**The data:** Shape's list of boards and modules (63 of them: 8 Arduino, 4 Raspberry Pi, 10 ESP32 and
ESP8266, 7 other microcontrollers, 10 power, 9 motor and relay, 15 sensors and other modules):

- name, extra search words, size, mounting holes and screw size;
- the ports at each edge, with the height of each above the board;
- Shape's "check" flag where sizes vary between makers. The card then says "sizes vary between
  makers: measure yours".

**Pins** (Shape doesn't have them): a module sits on a perfboard by its pins, so Bench needs to know
where they are. In order:

1. KiCad's footprint, where KiCad has one: Arduino Nano and Uno, Adafruit Feather, Raspberry Pi Pico,
   Pi Zero, Maple Mini, Pololu breakouts and a few more. These are covered by the KiCad credit Bench
   already carries.
2. Our own pin rows from the makers' drawings, for the most common boards KiCad lacks: ESP32 DevKit
   (30 and 38 pins), D1 mini, XIAO, LM2596, MT3608, TP4056.
3. No pins for the rest. Bench places these on the project's outline for the part, the way it places
   modules today.

**The models (my call):** one small GLB per module, made by a tool in `tools/` from the data:

- a board in its usual colour, with holes through it;
- a block for each port;
- header pins where the pins are known.

This is the same approach as our own MP1584 model, and the models are MIT like it. The library must
stay under its 8 MB budget, so each model aims for under 8 KB, about 0.5 MB for all 63. Repeated pins
are one shape placed per copy, as the converter already does.

**Not in 1.0:** Shape's 40 batteries (packs in strap trays belong to the box, 2.0), and Shape's
openings list (also the box).

## 4. C. Send to Shape

**What the builder gets:**

- **Where:** Menu → **Send to Shape…**, and the same item in the 3D view's save menu.
- **What it shows:** a short dialog with what will be sent. Every field is filled in and can be
  changed:
  - **Which board:** the drawing's board, or My board when you have placed parts on yours.
  - **Name:** the project's name.
  - **Size:** from the board's holes plus one hole of board round the edge. You can type the real size.
    The owner's board is a 7 × 9 cm electrokit board, which the project file doesn't record.
  - **Mounting holes:** none (Shape then holds the board by its edges), four in the corners a set
    distance in from each edge, or typed.
  - **Screw size:** M2, M2.5, M3 or M4.
  - **Height:** the tallest part on top, measured from Bench's 3D parts the way 0.9's lid check does.
  - **Ports at an edge:** USB, DC jack, network, HDMI, audio jack, SD card. Each is a connector on top
    within 8 mm of an edge, named the way Shape names them.
  - **On top:** LEDs, push buttons, pots, encoders, slide and toggle switches, buzzers. Shape cuts a
    hole in the wall or lid in front of each one.
  - Pin headers and screw terminals are not openings in Shape. The dialog lists them as "wires leave
    the board here", so the builder knows why the fume extractor's fans, LCD and pot don't show up.
- **Send** puts the board in Shape's Your parts. In Shape it's then Project box → Board mounts → your
  board.
- **Sending again** replaces the same board rather than adding a copy (one id per project and board).

**How:** Shape keeps Your parts in browser storage under `makerforge.parts`, up to 40 boards. Each board
is a short list: name, size, holes, screw, ports, the parts on top and height. Shape checks and limits
every number when it reads the list, so a bad entry can't break it. Bench writes in exactly that form.
This is the suite's "shared storage" convention working as planned. Bench's own keys stay `bench.*`;
this one key belongs to Shape.

**Where it works:** only where Bench and Shape share an address, forge.retr0plus.se. Under dev.py, or on
another host, the dialog says that in plain words and sends nothing. Features must work on the website,
and this one does.

**Limits:**

- If Shape's Your parts is full (40 boards), the dialog says so and sends nothing.
- Sending isn't an undo step in Bench, because it changes Shape's list, not your project. Shape's Your
  parts can remove the board again.

## 5. The rest of 1.0 (from the roadmap, detailed before the plan)

- Security review of the cleaner and the browser rules.
- Test on a real phone and a real tablet.
- A user manual inside the app.
- Live at `forge.retr0plus.se/bench/`, listed on the Workbench page. That work happens in the website
  repo.
- "Report a problem with this drawing": a new GitHub issue on the project's repo, with the sheet and
  part filled in.

## 6. Constraints

- Bench's rules stand:
  - strict browser rules (deploy/csp.txt unchanged);
  - Trusted Types, and pages built only with `el()`, never innerHTML;
  - undo through `history.do`;
  - everything works on the website with nothing installed.
- Shape is one large file, and some of its screens build pages in ways Bench forbids. Its data tables
  and calculations come over as copies. Its screens are rebuilt Bench's way.
- No change to Shape's repo for 1.0. Any later Shape work goes through its own branch and pull request.
- The library stays under 8 MB.

## 7. Approaches considered

- **Wire lengths:**
  - Shape's box-wire rule (plus 10 %, plus 20 mm) or a perfboard rule. **Picked:** the perfboard rule
    (§2), because links lie flat on the board.
- **Module models:**
  - (a) a GLB per module made by a tool; **picked**;
  - (b) drawn in the browser from the data;
  - (c) KiCad's models: KiCad has only three module models, none of them hobby boards.
  - Why (a): a GLB is something every app in the suite can load, Circuit and Shape included. (b) costs
    no files, but every app would need the drawing code. The cost of (a): about 0.5 MB of the
    library's budget.
- **Send to Shape:**
  - (a) shared browser storage; **picked**;
  - (b) a downloaded board file Shape already opens (a minimal KiCad board);
  - (c) wait for 2.0, when Shape reads `forge-project.json`.
  - Why (a): it needs nothing from Shape and nothing from the user. (b) would make Bench write KiCad
    files, against the "KiCad is import only" decision. (c) delays it a whole stage.

## 8. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

- **Goals:**
  - no more measuring links by hand;
  - modules look like themselves in 3D;
  - a board reaches Shape's box maker in one step.
- **Non-goals:**
  - wires off the board (to fans, the LCD, the pot), whose lengths depend on the box (2.0);
  - batteries;
  - Shape's openings list;
  - any change to Shape.
- **Anti-goals:**
  - a module drawn at a confident size that is wrong for the user's maker without saying so;
  - a link number that doesn't match the sheet;
  - Send to Shape quietly doing nothing where the addresses differ.

## 9. Left to the builder

- The colours per group.
- Whether the 5 mm end allowance needs a setting.
- The dialog's layout.
- Whether Wire to cut rows get a Show button that lights the link in 3D.
- A one-off end-to-end check before release: serve Shape's built `index.html` and Bench under one local
  address, send the fume extractor's board, and see it in Shape's Your parts. This is a manual check,
  not part of check.sh, because Shape is another repo.
