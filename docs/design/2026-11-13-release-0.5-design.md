# Bench 0.5 "Make it yours" — design

Date: 2026-11-13. The owner asked: "add 50 basic and medium improvements and 10 big improvements".
It builds on 0.4 (Your board, from your photos) and 0.4.1 (basics). The rules are the same as
every release:
- No markup strings, and project or progress data is untrusted and checked on read.
- Everything you change can be undone and is saved.
- New progress fields are optional, so older files still open.
- The CSP stays as it is.

## The 10 big improvements

**B1 Move parts on your photo.** Drag a placed part's mark on a lined-up photo. The whole part
moves together, snaps to holes, and one move is one undo step. The part has to be selected first,
so a stray drag doesn't move anything.

**B2 Check my board.** Live checks of your own placement, listed in the Your board section:
- two legs in one hole;
- two parts' bodies on top of each other, using the typical body sizes the 3D view uses;
- two legs on the same hole column bridged by a body. This one is not checked: it needs strips,
  and a perfboard has none.
- legs of different lines on neighbouring holes, as a warning to solder carefully;
- parts not placed yet.

Each finding names the parts and rings them on the photo when clicked. This is a focused port of
the ideas in `pcb.py`, which runs on the project's own layout, for a board you laid out yourself.

**B3 Suggested wires on your board.** For each line with two or more placed legs:
- **The route:** Bench joins the legs with the shortest tree (Prim's algorithm on hole distance).
  Each join is drawn as an L-shaped wire underneath.
- **Choices:** two shapes per line, columns first or rows first, and a straight line as a third.
- **Showing:** the wires are drawn on lined-up photos, underneath ones mirrored, coloured by line
  kind. What to wire's line gets "Show wires on my photos".
- **Text:** "U2 pin 7 to C10 +: 3 holes down, 6 across".
- The choice per line is saved.

**B4 Your board in 3D.** The 3D view can show your own placement instead of sheet 4 (a switch
on the 3D bar).
- **How:** your placements are turned into board data: each placed part's pins in your frame,
  types and sizes from the project, and the board sized to fit.
- **Wiring:** the suggested wires (B3) are drawn underneath.
- **Before placing:** a part not placed yet isn't drawn, and the 3D bar says how many are
  missing.

**B5 Parts library.** A built-in library (`src/library.js`, data with its sources) for common parts:
the ATmega328P, the MP1584 buck module, 2N2222 and BC547 transistors, the 1N4007 and 1N5402
diodes, LEDs, electrolytics, a 16 MHz crystal, potentiometers and 4-pin fan headers.
- **What it holds:** pin function names (for example `PD2 (INT0)` and `AVCC`), which way round,
  a short how-to and typical body sizes.
- **Matching:** by value or part name, for example "ATmega328P-PU" → `atmega328p`.
- **Where it shows:** the pin view shows the names beside the line labels. The card gets "How to
  use it" and the library's polarity note, marked "from the Bench library".

**B6 Guided continuity test.** The multimeter's beep test, one probe pair at a time:
- **Should beep:** each leg against the next on the same line ("U2 pin 7 to C10 +").
- **Must not beep:** each chip pin against its neighbour when they're on different lines, a short
  between pins being the most common perfboard fault. The supplies against 0 V go here too.
- **Answering:** Beeped or No beep. A wrong answer is kept as a fault with what it means
  ("short between U2 pin 7 and pin 8: look for a solder bridge").
- **Progress:** saved, undoable, and listed as "N of M done, K faults".

**B7 3D: accurate models.** Every part is drawn from its datasheet sizes instead of placeholder
blocks. The sizes live in the library (B5), each with its source.
- **Passives:** resistors with their real colour bands (from the value) and legs bent at the body.
  Ceramic discs printed with their code ("104").
- **Electrolytics:** a sleeve, a − stripe with its marks, an aluminium top with the vent cross, and
  the longer + leg.
- **The DIP chip:** body, notch, dot, printed name and shouldered pins. Your two female header
  strips are drawn as what they are.
- **Small parts:**
  - TO-92: the D-shaped body, flat face and three legs;
  - LED: the dome with its flange and flat side, and the long anode leg;
  - the HC-49 crystal;
  - DO-41 and DO-201AD diodes with their bands.
- **Connectors:** pin headers with plastic base and square pins, and the white 4-pin fan header
  with its key.
- **The MP1584 module:** board, inductor, chip, trimmer and pads.

The shapes use three.js r128's lathe, extrude and tube geometry. Printed text is a canvas texture.

**B8 3D: a better look, easier to use.**
- **The look:**
  - studio lighting built from a small light-box scene through `PMREMGenerator`, so metal shines;
  - ACES tone mapping and soft shadows on the board;
  - FR-4 green with copper pads and drilled holes, and solder joints on every leg;
  - shiny bare-wire buses and coloured insulated links.
- **Moving around:**
  - a view cube (click a face or corner);
  - buttons for Fit, Top, Underneath, Front, Side and 3/4;
  - smooth camera moves (instant with Reduce motion), and the arrow keys orbit.
- **Picking:**
  - hover highlights a part with its name and value;
  - click selects;
  - double-click flies to it.

**B9 3D: tools.**
- **Seeing inside:**
  - an exploded view: parts lift off the board on their legs, a slider sets how far;
  - X-ray: the board goes see-through, to follow the wiring underneath;
  - a cut-away: a plane through the board you can move.
- **Building:** step playback places the parts in build order, one step at a time.
- **Measuring:** click two points for the distance in mm and in holes.
- **Saving:** a picture (PNG) and the board as STL (for Shape or a slicer).

**B10 History: named versions and the build log.**
- **Named versions:** save all your progress under a name, restore it with undo, delete (asks
  twice); at most 20, in the browser, per project.
- **Build log:** a timeline of what you did with times (soldered, wired, steps, notes, placements,
  continuity), time spent today and in total, "Copy as text", and when a part was soldered on its
  card.

(Touch support and the command palette move to the 50 below: 51 and 52, as extras.)

## The 50 improvements

### Moving around
1. Links to a sheet and part (`?sheet=SFE-04&part=R8`), and "Copy link" on the card.
2. Back and forward through the parts you opened (Alt+← / Alt+→, and buttons on the card).
3. `[` and `]` pick the previous and next sheet.
4. `w` fits the sheet's width.
5. Zoom and position are remembered per sheet across visits.
6. Tabs show each sheet's soldered count ("3/12").
7. Hovering a part's label on a drawing shows its value and job.
8. Find remembers your recent searches.

### The part card
9. Resistor colour bands drawn from the value.
10. Capacitor markings from the value ("100 nF is marked 104").
11. A polarity picture for electrolytics, diodes and LEDs.
12. "Copy" copies the ref and value.
13. Previous and next part in checklist order (`,` and `.`).
14. "Mark all its pins wired".
15. "Search the web" for the part. It opens a search only when you click it.
16. When it was soldered ("Soldered 14:05").
17. "Tick all 5 parts with this value".

### Checklist
18. A search box.
19. Group by type (resistors, capacitors…) with counts.
20. Sort by name or by build step.
21. Each part's step number in the list.
22. `Ctrl+S` saves the progress file.

### What to wire
23. A filter box for lines.
24. "Mark all wired" on a line.
25. A summary by kind: supplies, 0 V, signals.
26. "Next line not finished".

### Steps
27. Moving on by itself when a step is finished (a switch).
28. About how many joints a step has.
29. Your note on each step.
30. Print just this step.

### Your board
31. Show or hide the marks on the photo.
32. Line labels beside the chip's pins on the photo (a switch).
33. Mark size: small, medium or large.
34. "Next part to place", in build order.
35. Clear my board (asks twice, undoable).
36. The open step's parts not placed yet.
37. The selected part's pin names on the photo.
38. Soldered parts' marks are filled in.

### 3D board
39. "Build up to this step" hides the later steps' parts.
40. Save a picture of the 3D board.
41. Labels on and off.
42. Front and side views.
43. A line's "Show" also lights its parts in 3D.

### The app
44. Compact mode in Easy reading.
45. Drag to resize the side panel (remembered).
46. "What's new" once after an update.
47. Build report: a summary to download (Markdown).
48. A tab flashes when its sheet changes.
49. "Try again" when a sheet fails to load.
50. Calculators: Ohm's law, LED resistor, voltage divider.

Extras:
51. Touch: pinch to zoom, two fingers to pan, bigger targets on touch screens, the panel below the
    drawing on narrow screens.
52. Command palette (Ctrl+K): parts, lines, values, sheets, steps and actions in one search.

## Order of work

The owner asked for the 3D to be "much better: more accurate models, easier to use and much more
functions", so the 3D work comes second:
- **A:** library, card helpers (9–17).
- **3D:** B7, B8, B9, B4, and 39–43.
- **B:** continuity, steps (27–30).
- **C:** drag, check, wires, your board (31–38).
- **E:** history (B10), report.
- **F:** palette, moving around (1–8, 22).
- **G:** touch, lists (18–21, 23–26), the app (44–50).

**Engine:** Bench stays on three.js r128, the same as Maker Forge Shape. Bench keeps its own copy
because its CSP allows no outside scripts. The website has its own small WebGL STL viewer.
What's new is written in Bench without downloading more three.js files:
- the studio light box for `PMREMGenerator`;
- the STL writer;
- the view cube.

So it can go to Shape later through its PR routine.
