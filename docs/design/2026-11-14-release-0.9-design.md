# Bench 0.9 "Real parts" — design

Date: 2026-11-14. The owner asked to "import a lot more different parts in 3D from any available
library and use them in bench and inventory app not just bench", to "improve our 3d viewer in any
every single useful logical cool and easy to use way", and for Bench (with the rest of the suite)
"to end up with the ability to produce a full board from scratch" with many KiCad and SolidWorks
features. They picked "both halves" for 3D (whole KiCad boards through KiCad's own export, plus
common parts converted and shipped) and agreed the order in `ROADMAP.md` ("Where this is going").
This release is step 1 of that order.

## Intent and the why

Bench draws every part with its own hand-made shapes (0.5, 0.8). They're close, but there are only
about a dozen kinds. KiCad ships thousands of accurate 3D parts under a licence that lets us share
them with a credit. Using them gives Bench real parts. It also starts the **Maker Forge Library**,
the one set of parts Parts, Circuit and Shape will share later. And it lets Bench open the boards
people already make in KiCad. Then KiCad's error checks become something a beginner can follow:
**Show me**, and **Ask AI how to fix**.

## Goals

1. Real 3D parts from KiCad's library for Bench's own boards (the fume extractor and any
   `forge-project.json` board).
2. Open a KiCad project directly: part cards, connections, a 3D board, build steps. In the browser
   alone, and fuller with `dev.py` and KiCad installed.
3. KiCad's schematic check (ERC) and board check (DRC) in plain words, with Show me and Ask AI.
4. A better 3D view all round (§5).
5. The library laid out so it can move to its own home, unchanged, once Circuit needs it.

## Non-goals for 0.9

- Drawing a schematic from `.kicad_sch` in the browser. Sheets come from KiCad's own SVG export
  (with `dev.py`), or from exports the repo already has.
- STEP files (they need OpenCascade compiled for the browser, about 10 MB; later, for Shape).
- Bench's own ERC or DRC. KiCad's are used.
- Editing or writing KiCad files. That's Circuit.
- The wiring harness, the board in its box, AR on phones (later stages).
- The guided continuity test on KiCad boards (1.4, with "Highlight a connection").

## Anti-goals (a failure even if it works)

- Bench opens slower. Nothing from the library loads until the 3D view opens.
- The fume extractor looks worse or works differently. Its tests must pass unchanged.
- Models shipped without KiCad's credit and licence.
- Anything uploaded, or `dev.py` writing into the user's project folder.
- KiCad becoming required for anything that works today.

## Constraints

- Bench's rules stay: strict CSP, Trusted Types, pages built with `el()`, no `eval`, undo for every
  change. three.js stays at r128 (`src/three.module.js`).
- The website is static. `kicad-cli` can only run through `dev.py` on the user's computer.
- KiCad's 3D models are **CC-BY-SA 4.0 with an exception** that leaves designs using them free. The
  converted files keep that licence, in their own folder with a credit and a note that they were
  converted. Bench's code stays MIT.
- **One download needs the owner's yes:** three.js 0.128.0's `GLTFLoader.js` and `GLTFExporter.js`
  (MIT, the same release as the vendored three.js), from the npm package. Nothing like them is on
  this computer.
- Phones: the shipped models stay small (§1 budget) and load only when used.
- `bash check.sh` stays green, with new Python, unit and browser tests.

## Approaches

**3D (decided by the owner, 2026-11-14):**
1. **Both halves (chosen):** whole KiCad boards through `kicad-cli pcb export glb`, plus about 150
   common through-hole models converted once and shipped. Works on the website for Bench's own
   boards, and fully with KiCad installed.
2. Read models from the user's KiCad folder when needed. Nothing shipped, but useless without KiCad,
   so it's no good on the website or a phone.
3. STEP through OpenCascade compiled for the browser: the most exact, but about 10 MB to load and
   slow. Better kept for Shape and the board in its box.

**Model format (my call): convert KiCad's `.wrl` to `.glb` ahead of time, in Python.** GLB is small
and binary, and it's what `kicad-cli` writes for whole boards, so one loader serves both. Parsing
VRML in the browser would need a large parser for every visitor. KiCad's `.wrl` files are all
written by the same exporter ("kicad StepUp wrl exported"): a list of materials, then shapes made
of faces. That makes a converter short.

**Where the library lives (my call): inside Bench, as plain files under `src/library/`.** Built so
it moves as one folder once Circuit needs it. A separate repo now would be a suite framework built
ahead of need.

**Reading KiCad projects (my call): both ways.** Bench always reads KiCad's text files itself
(parts, connections, footprint places), so a GitHub link works on the website. With `dev.py` and
KiCad installed, `kicad-cli` adds the sheets, the exact 3D board and the checks.

## 1. The Maker Forge Library, first version

**Files:**
- `src/library/index.json`: every part (below).
- `src/library/models/<id>.glb`: the converted models.
- `src/library/LICENSE-KICAD-3D.md`: CC-BY-SA 4.0 with KiCad's exception, the credit, the KiCad
  version the models came from (9.0.6) and what was changed ("converted to GLB, faces merged by
  material, colours kept").
- `src/library/README.md`: how to add a part.
- `tools/kicad3d.py` and `tools/kicad3d-parts.txt`: the converter and the list it converts.

**An entry in `index.json`:**

```json
{ "id": "r-axial-din0207-p10.16-h", "name": "Resistor, ¼ W, lying flat, 10.16 mm",
  "kind": "res", "mount": "tht",
  "footprint": { "pins": [[0, 0], [10.16, 0]], "pitch": 2.54, "drill": 0.8 },
  "model": { "file": "models/r-axial-din0207-p10.16-h.glb", "bytes": 31000,
             "box": [[-1.3, -1.3, 0], [11.5, 1.3, 2.6]], "body": "RES-THT-01" },
  "paint": "bands",
  "source": { "from": "KiCad 9.0.6", "path": "Resistor_THT.3dshapes/R_Axial_DIN0207_L6.3mm_D2.5mm_P10.16mm_Horizontal.wrl",
              "licence": "CC-BY-SA-4.0 WITH KiCad-libraries-exception" } }
```

Pin positions are in mm from pin 1, x to the right and y up as seen from above. `body` names the
material Bench paints on (§2). Symbols, datasheets and how-to pages join the entries with Parts and
Circuit. Today's how-to data in `src/library.js` stays where it is, linked by `id`, until the library
moves out.

**`tools/kicad3d.py`** (no libraries beyond Python's own):
- Reads each `.wrl` from the KiCad folder given on the command line (`--kicad D:\KiCad`), parses the
  materials (`DEF name Material { diffuseColor … transparency … }`) and the shapes
  (`material USE name`, `Coordinate { point […] }`, `coordIndex […]`).
- Splits each face into triangles, merges every shape of one material into one mesh, scales by 2.54
  (one VRML unit is 0.1 inch), and keeps KiCad's frame: origin at pin 1, Z up.
- Welds points closer than 0.001 mm, and computes normals that keep edges sharp beyond 30°.
- Reads the matching footprint too (`footprints/<Library>.pretty/<name>.kicad_mod`), for each pad's
  number, position and drill. Footprints count y downwards and 3D models upwards, so y is turned
  over. The entry's pins are the through-hole pads in number order.
- Materials: colour and transparency from VRML. Metal or not from the material's name (`PIN-`,
  `MET-` are metal), roughness from `shininess`.
- Writes a GLB (positions, normals, indices, one material per mesh) and the entry with its box and
  size. The output is the same bytes every time, so git diffs stay quiet.
- `check.sh` checks that `index.json` and `models/` match and that the licence file is there. It
  doesn't need KiCad to run.

**Which models (about 150), from `kicad-3dmodels`:** resistors lying flat (P7.62–15.24) and standing
(P2.54, P5.08); ceramic discs and dipped (P2.54, P5.00); electrolytics D5–D10 at their usual
pitches; film boxes; DO-35, DO-41 and DO-201AD diodes; 3 mm and 5 mm LEDs; TO-92 (inline and
wide); TO-220 standing and lying; DIP-8 to DIP-40, each plain and in a socket; pin headers and
female strips 1×2 to 1×20 and 2×N; 2-, 3- and 4-way screw terminals (5.08 and 3.5 mm); HC-49 and
HC-49/S crystals; trimmers and 9 mm pots; 6 mm and 12 mm buttons; Molex KK and JST XH 2–6 pin; a
DC barrel jack; USB-B and micro-USB through-hole; a buzzer; a small relay; slide switches.

**Budget:** 8 MB for all of them together, 250 KB for one. About 70 KB of `.wrl` comes out near
30 KB of GLB. A part over 250 KB (the DIP sockets' inner contacts) drops the faces that can't be
seen from outside, or stays out of the library and keeps Bench's own model.

## 2. Bench uses the models

**Matching a Bench part to a model** (`src/library-match.js`, data in, an `id` out, unit-tested):
1. A `model` field in the project or in Looks like (a library `id`, or a KiCad model path) wins.
2. Otherwise by type and size: `res` by mount and hole span (the nearest library pitch); `cer` by
   style; `elec` by D and pitch; diodes by value (1N400x → DO-41, 1N540x → DO-201AD, 1N4148 →
   DO-35); `led` by 3 or 5 mm; `to92`; `to220` by facing; `dip` by pin count and holder (socket or
   not); headers by count, rows and female; crystals; pots; buttons; terminals.
3. No match (the MP1584 module, underside parts, links): Bench's own model, as today.

**Placing it:** pin 1 of the model goes on the part's pin-1 hole, turned so the model's pin 2 lies
towards the part's pin 2, and mirrored for parts under the board. Which of Bench's pin names is
KiCad's pin 1 follows KiCad's rules: on diodes and LEDs it's the cathode (K); on electrolytics it's
+; chips and headers go by number; three-leg parts in the order the project lists their pins. If the model's pins miss the
part's holes by more than 0.3 mm (a footprint that doesn't fit), Bench keeps its own model for that
part and says so in the 3D info.

**Painting what KiCad leaves plain:** the model's `body` material is tinted, and Bench's own
markings go on top: resistor colour bands (KiCad's resistor body is one colour); the
electrolytic's printed value and − stripe; the LED's colour; the chip's printed name. The bands
reuse 0.8's band rings, spaced along the model's body length from its box.

**Real or simple:** a **Parts: Real · Simple** switch in the 3D toolbar, remembered. Real is the
default. Simple is today's Bench models (lighter, and the fallback). The 3D info says how many
parts are real.

**Loading:** models load only when the 3D view opens, several at a time, cached by the browser.
Bench draws its simple model first and swaps each real one in as it arrives, so the view is never
empty. A thin bar says "Real parts 12 of 31".

**Looks like** gets **3D model:** the library models that fit the part's holes (the same pin count,
pins within 0.3 mm), each a button that swaps it in the 3D view, undoable. Also **Use my own
model…**: a `.glb` file from your computer, up to 5 MB, kept in this browser's own database
(IndexedDB, since it's too big for the settings store) and not in the progress file, which says
so. With `dev.py`, a `.wrl` works too: `dev.py` converts it with the
same converter.

## 3. Open a KiCad project

**Finding it:** a folder or repo with a `.kicad_pro` is a KiCad project. The file lists (`dev.py`,
a picked folder, GitHub) now include `.kicad_pro`, `.kicad_sch` and `.kicad_pcb`, read as text, up
to 20 MB each. If the folder also has a `forge-project.json`, that wins, and KiCad fills what it
leaves out. A repo with several KiCad projects opens the first by name, and the Menu offers the
others.

**Read in the browser, always** (`src/kicad.js`):
- A small reader for KiCad's bracketed text: strings with escapes, numbers, words, lists. It has
  limits on depth (200), size and count, with tests against broken and nasty files, like the SVG
  cleaner's.
- From `.kicad_pcb`:
  - each footprint: reference, value, footprint name, position, rotation, side, through-hole or
    surface-mount;
  - its pads: number, position, drill and **net name** (`(net 2 "GND")`);
  - its 3D model path with offset, scale and rotation. Footprints name the `.step` file under
    `${KICAD9_3DMODEL_DIR}` (6, 7 and 8 in older files) or `${KIPRJMOD}`. So matching a library
    model ignores the folder variable and the extension;
  - the board outline from `Edge.Cuts` (lines, arcs, circles, rectangles), and the thickness.
- From `.kicad_sch` and its sub-sheets: each symbol's reference, value, footprint, datasheet,
  library name, "not fitted" (DNP) and "leave out of the parts list". So a schematic alone still
  gives part cards and a parts list.
- **What Bench makes of it:**
  - Part cards: the value, the type guessed from the library and footprint names (`R_`, `C_`, `CP_`,
    `D_`, `LED_`, `Q_`, `U_`, `J_`…), the datasheet button, and **what each pin connects to**, from
    the pad nets.
  - The checklist, with "not fitted" parts listed apart.
  - **Build steps made for you**, when the project has none: surface-mount parts first, then
    through-hole parts from the lowest up (links; resistors and diodes lying flat; chip sockets;
    small capacitors; transistors and regulators; LEDs; headers; electrolytics; connectors and big
    parts). One step per group, and adjustable as in 0.8.
  - The panel tabs show what applies. A copper board has nothing to hand-wire, so Wiring hides,
    as 0.8 already does for empty tabs. The Board tab's photo tools stay for perfboard projects.

**With `dev.py` and KiCad, more** (`dev.py --kicad PATH`, otherwise found on PATH or in KiCad's
usual folders on Windows, macOS and Linux):
- Bench asks `dev.py` for what it needs (`/api/kicad/<what>`), when it needs it: the sheets and
  the checks when the project opens, and the 3D board when the 3D view opens. `dev.py` runs
  `kicad-cli`:
  - `sch export svg`: one SVG per sheet, shown as normal Bench sheets (Find part works, 0.1 §11);
  - `pcb export svg`: the board's top and bottom (copper, silkscreen, outline) as layout sheets,
    so Flip works;
  - `pcb export glb --subst-models --include-tracks --include-pads --include-zones`, plus the
    silkscreen and solder mask flags if KiCad 9 offers them: the exact 3D board;
  - `sch erc --format json --severity-all` and
    `pcb drc --format json --schematic-parity --severity-all`: the checks (§4).
- **Factory files ★ along the way:** a **Make factory files** button runs `pcb export gerbers`,
  `drill`, `pos` and `sch export bom` and hands back one zip, ready to upload to a board maker.
  The owner asked early on for files to send to manufacturers, and with KiCad there this costs
  little. Circuit's factory pack later reuses it.
- **The cache is outside the project:** `%LOCALAPPDATA%\MakerForgeBench\kicad` (or
  `~/.cache/maker-forge-bench/kicad`), keyed by the project's path and each input file's size and
  time. It's trimmed past 500 MB. Nothing is written into the project folder.
- **Safely:** `kicad-cli` runs with a list of arguments (no shell), only on files inside the
  opened folder, one job at a time, with a 120-second limit. While it works, Bench says what it's
  doing ("KiCad is making the 3D board, about 15 s"). When a KiCad file changes, the outputs are
  made again and the page refreshes, as live reload does today.
- On the website, without `dev.py`, Bench uses any SVG or PDF exports the repo has, plus what it
  reads itself. It says once, where it matters: "Run Bench on your computer with KiCad for the
  sheets, the exact 3D board and the checks."

**The 3D board of a KiCad project:**
- **From `kicad-cli`:** the GLB. Its parts are nodes named by reference (55 of 63 on KiCad's
  `pic_programmer` demo; the other 8 have no model). Bench makes each node a part, so picking,
  hover, step highlighting, explode, X-ray, cut-away, measure and find all work as on Bench's own
  boards. The GLB's scale (glTF uses metres) is turned into Bench's millimetres.
- **Built by Bench, without `kicad-cli`:** the outline extruded to the board's thickness in its
  colour, copper rings for the pads, and each footprint's model from the Maker Forge Library,
  matched by its model file name and placed with the footprint's offset, turn and scale.
  Footprints with no library model get a grey box the size of their courtyard, 3 mm tall,
  labelled "no 3D model".
- **Show a connection:** a net in a part card ("GND") lights every pad on it in 3D.

## 4. Errors found, and help fixing them

- The Checks tab gets **KiCad checks**: the ERC and DRC findings, errors first, then warnings.
  Each has a plain-words title from a table of KiCad's common types, with KiCad's own description
  under it. For example: `pin_not_connected` "A pin is connected to nothing";
  `power_pin_not_driven` "Nothing powers this power pin (KiCad wants a PWR_FLAG here)";
  `clearance` "Copper too close together"; `unconnected_items` "Not joined yet";
  `footprint_symbol_mismatch` "The board and the schematic disagree about this part". Unknown
  types show KiCad's words.
- **Show me:** an ERC finding opens its sheet, zooms to the spot and rings it. A DRC finding marks
  the spot on the board sheet or in 3D, and lights the parts it names.
  - *Found while checking:* KiCad 9.0.6's ERC file says millimetres, but gave 0.3175 for a symbol
    at 31.75 mm on the `pic_programmer` demo: 100 times too small.
  - DRC positions were right. Bench checks ERC positions against the symbols they name, and scales
    them when they're off by 100.
  - KiCad's sheet SVGs measure in millimetres in the schematic's own frame (`viewBox="0 0 297
    210"` for A4), so a corrected position lands straight on the sheet.
- **Ask AI how to fix:** sends the finding, the parts it names and their connections (nothing
  else) to the AI set up in Easy reading (your Claude key or Ollama, 0.7). It asks for a fix in
  plain words, shown as advice. Bench never changes the files.
- **Ignore this one:** like KiCad's exclusions. Kept with your progress, and undoable.
- On the website, without `dev.py`, the section says how to get the checks.

## 5. A better 3D view

1. **Truer light and materials:** an environment light made in code (three r128's
   `PMREMGenerator` over a simple sky-and-floor scene), so metal looks like metal and plastic like
   plastic. A soft shadow under each part, turned off by Less motion and on screens under 760 px
  wide.
2. **Board colours:** green, black, blue, red, white, purple, plus perfboard (brown, copper pads)
   and stripboard (copper strips) for Bench's own boards. In the 3D toolbar, kept per project.
   KiCad boards start in their solder mask colour when the file gives one.
3. **Your progress in 3D:** parts you've ticked show shiny solder joints underneath; the rest show
   bare legs.
4. **Which way round:** a toggle that floats a mark over each polarized part (the + leg, the
   cathode band, pin 1).
5. **The camera follows the guide:** opening a step turns the 3D view to that step's parts.
6. **Find in 3D:** a part found in Find lights up in 3D, and the camera flies to it.
7. **Saved views:** name the current view and keep up to nine, per project.
8. **Height check:** the tallest part on each side and its height, plus a "lid at __ mm" plane that
   shows what pokes through. Ready for the board in its box.
9. **Check my board in 3D:** its findings as red marks on the 3D board; clicking one opens the
   finding.
10. **Export:** GLB (the whole scene, real parts, through three's `GLTFExporter`) and OBJ (written
    by Bench, it's short), next to today's PNG and STL.
11. **Turntable video:** a 10-second turn of the board saved as WebM (the browser's own recorder;
    hidden where the browser can't record).
12. **Loading bar** for real parts and for KiCad's 3D board.

## 6. Tests

- **Python** (`tests/test_kicad3d.py`, `tests/test_dev.py`):
  - the converter on a tiny `.wrl` fixture (two materials, a square and a triangle): ×2.54, the
    frame, a valid GLB, the same bytes twice;
  - `index.json` matches `models/`, and the licence file is there;
  - `dev.py` finds `kicad-cli` (with a fake script standing in), the cache key changes with the
    files, paths outside the folder are refused, and the time limit works.
- **Unit** (node):
  - the bracketed-text reader, with nasty files;
  - `.kicad_pcb` footprints, pads, nets and outline;
  - `.kicad_sch` symbols across sub-sheets;
  - the model matching table, and ERC/DRC JSON to findings, with the ×100 fix;
  - the build-order rule.
- **A test KiCad project** written by hand in KiCad 9's format: about eight parts (resistors, a
  disc and an electrolytic, an LED, a DIP-8 in a socket, a header, a screw terminal), one sheet,
  one board. `kicad-cli` must open it and check it cleanly. Its exports (sheet SVGs, board SVGs,
  GLB, ERC and DRC JSON) are saved beside it, so the browser tests run without KiCad. `dev.py`
  reads them from there in test mode.
- **Browser:**
  - opening the test project: the sheets, the parts named in 3D, nets on part cards, KiCad checks
    with Show me, and the build steps made for you;
  - the same project read by the browser alone (no KiCad outputs);
  - the fume extractor in Real and in Simple;
  - the Looks like model picker, your own GLB, board colours, GLB export;
  - the phone layout, and no page errors.
- **Existing tests:** the harness picks Simple parts unless a test asks for Real, the way it seeds
  the All tab today. So every existing 3D test keeps checking what it checks now.

## 7. Order of work

1. The converter, the models, the index and the licence file. The loader, after the download is
   approved.
2. Bench uses the models: matching, placing, painting, Real or Simple, Looks like.
3. Reading KiCad projects in the browser, and the 3D board built by Bench.
4. `dev.py` with `kicad-cli`: sheets, the exact 3D board, the checks with Show me and Ask AI, and
   factory files.
5. The 3D view upgrades (§5), mostly after step 2.
6. Manual, What's new, README, ROADMAP "done", and "As built" here.

## 8. Left to the builder

The exact `kicad-cli` flags for each export (checked against `--help` on 9.0.6), the GLB scale and
axes, the final list of about 150 file names, the material-name rules, the full plain-words table
for ERC and DRC types, and where each new control sits. Follow 0.8's patterns: the Menu, the panel
tabs, and the 3D toolbar.

## Calls I made (the owner can change any)

- The library starts inside Bench (`src/library/`), ready to move.
- Models converted ahead of time to GLB in Python; no VRML parser in the browser.
- Real parts by default, with a Real/Simple switch; tests use Simple unless they ask.
- An 8 MB budget for all shipped models, 250 KB per model.
- Your own models: GLB in the browser (5 MB, this browser only); `.wrl` too with `dev.py`; STEP
  later.
- `forge-project.json` wins over KiCad files when both are there.
- The KiCad cache lives outside the project folder.
- Build steps made for you, lowest parts first, when a KiCad project has none.
- Factory files for KiCad boards added to 0.9 (small with KiCad there; can wait for Circuit).
- The continuity test on KiCad boards waits for 1.4.
- A hand-written test KiCad project, with its KiCad outputs saved beside it.
