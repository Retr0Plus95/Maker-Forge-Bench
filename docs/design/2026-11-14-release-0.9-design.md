# Bench 0.9 "Real parts" — design

Date: 2026-11-14. The owner asked to "import a lot more different parts in 3D from any available
library and use them in bench and inventory app not just bench", to "improve our 3d viewer in any
every single useful logical cool and easy to use way", and for Bench (with the rest of the suite)
"to end up with the ability to produce a full board from scratch" with many KiCad and SolidWorks
features. They picked "both halves" for 3D (whole KiCad boards through KiCad's own export, plus
common parts converted and shipped) and agreed the order in `ROADMAP.md` ("Where this is going").
This release is step 1 of that order.

**Changed the same day:** the owner has never used KiCad and doesn't want Bench to depend on
running anything on their computer. Opening KiCad projects became an option "for people importing
their projects from there". It's designed in `docs/design/2026-11-14-kicad-import-design.md` and
planned for 1.2. So 0.9 is the real parts and the better 3D view, and **all of it works on the
website, with nothing running on your computer.**

## Intent and the why

Bench draws every part with its own hand-made shapes (0.5, 0.8). They're close, but there are only
about a dozen kinds. KiCad ships thousands of accurate 3D parts under a licence that lets us share
them with a credit. Using them gives Bench real parts. It also starts the **Maker Forge Library**,
the one set of parts Parts, Circuit and Shape will share later. The 3D view gets the rest of what
makes it useful at the bench: your progress, which way round, saved views, a height check for the
case, and ways to share it.

## Goals

1. Real 3D parts from KiCad's library for Bench's own boards (the fume extractor and any
   `forge-project.json` board), on the website and on a phone.
2. A better 3D view all round (§3).
3. The library laid out so it can move to its own home, unchanged, once Circuit needs it. KiCad
   import (1.2) uses the same library for KiCad boards.

## Non-goals for 0.9

- Opening KiCad projects, KiCad's checks and factory files: moved to 1.2 as an option (the KiCad
  import design).
- STEP files (they need OpenCascade compiled for the browser, about 10 MB; later, for Shape).
- The wiring harness, the board in its box, AR on phones (later stages).

## Anti-goals (a failure even if it works)

- Bench opens slower. Nothing from the library loads until the 3D view opens.
- The fume extractor looks worse or works differently. Its tests must pass unchanged.
- Models shipped without KiCad's credit and licence.
- Anything uploaded.
- Anything in 0.9 that needs Bench running on your computer, or KiCad installed. The one extra
  (`dev.py` turning a `.wrl` from your own project folder into a model) is a bonus, not a need.

## Constraints

- Bench's rules stay: strict CSP, Trusted Types, pages built with `el()`, no `eval`, undo for every
  change. three.js stays at r128 (`src/three.module.js`).
- KiCad's 3D models are **CC-BY-SA 4.0 with an exception** that leaves designs using them free. The
  converted files keep that licence, in their own folder with a credit and a note that they were
  converted. Bench's code stays MIT.
- **One download, approved by the owner and done (2026-11-14):** three.js 0.128.0's
  `GLTFLoader.js` and `GLTFExporter.js` (MIT, the same release as the vendored three.js), from the
  npm package. Checked: no `innerHTML` or `eval`, and they load only from Bench's own origin, so
  the CSP stays as it is.
- Phones: the shipped models stay small (§1 budget) and load only when used.
- `bash check.sh` stays green, with new Python, unit and browser tests.

## Approaches

**3D (decided by the owner, 2026-11-14):**
1. **Both halves (chosen):** whole KiCad boards through `kicad-cli pcb export glb`, plus about 150
   common through-hole models converted once and shipped. 0.9 builds the shipped half; whole
   KiCad boards come with KiCad import (1.2).
2. Read models from the user's KiCad folder when needed. Nothing shipped, but useless without KiCad,
   so it's no good on the website or a phone.
3. STEP through OpenCascade compiled for the browser: the most exact, but about 10 MB to load and
   slow. Better kept for Shape and the board in its box.

**Model format (my call): convert KiCad's `.wrl` to `.glb` ahead of time, in Python.** GLB is small
and binary, and it's what `kicad-cli` writes for whole boards, so one loader serves both. Parsing
VRML in the browser would need a large parser for every visitor. KiCad's `.wrl` files are all
written by the same exporter ("kicad StepUp wrl exported"): a list of materials, then shapes made
of faces. That makes a converter short.

**Where the library lives (my call): inside Bench, as plain files under `src/library/`.** Built so
it moves as one folder once Circuit needs it. A separate repo now would be a suite framework built
ahead of need.

## 1. The Maker Forge Library, first version

**Files:**
- `src/library/index.json`: every part (below).
- `src/library/models/<id>.glb`: the converted models.
- `src/library/LICENSE-KICAD-3D.md`: CC-BY-SA 4.0 with KiCad's exception, the credit, the KiCad
  version the models came from (9.0.6) and what was changed ("converted to GLB, faces merged by
  material, colours kept").
- `src/library/README.md`: how to add a part.
- `tools/kicad3d.py` and `tools/kicad3d-parts.tsv`: the converter and the list it converts.

**An entry in `index.json`:**

```json
{ "id": "r-axial-din0207-p10.16-h", "name": "Resistor, ¼ W, lying flat, 10.16 mm",
  "kind": "res", "mount": "tht",
  "footprint": { "pins": [[0, 0], [10.16, 0]], "pitch": 2.54, "drill": 0.8 },
  "model": { "file": "models/r-axial-din0207-p10.16-h.glb", "bytes": 31000,
             "box": [[-1.3, -1.3, 0], [11.5, 1.3, 2.6]], "body": "RES-THT-01" },
  "paint": "bands",
  "source": { "from": "KiCad 9.0.6", "path": "Resistor_THT.3dshapes/R_Axial_DIN0207_L6.3mm_D2.5mm_P10.16mm_Horizontal.wrl",
              "licence": "CC-BY-SA-4.0 WITH KiCad-libraries-exception" } }
```

Pin positions are in mm from pin 1, x to the right and y up as seen from above. `body` names the
material Bench paints on (§2). Symbols, datasheets and how-to pages join the entries with Parts and
Circuit. Today's how-to data in `src/library.js` stays where it is, linked by `id`, until the library
moves out.

**`tools/kicad3d.py`** (no libraries beyond Python's own):
- Reads each `.wrl` from the KiCad folder given on the command line (`--kicad D:\KiCad`), parses the
  materials (`DEF name Material { diffuseColor … transparency … }`) and the shapes
  (`material USE name`, `Coordinate { point […] }`, `coordIndex […]`).
- Splits each face into triangles, merges every shape of one material into one mesh, scales by 2.54
  (one VRML unit is 0.1 inch), and keeps KiCad's frame: origin at pin 1, Z up.
- Welds points closer than 0.001 mm, and computes normals that keep edges sharp beyond 30°.
- Reads the matching footprint too (`footprints/<Library>.pretty/<name>.kicad_mod`), for each pad's
  number, position and drill. Footprints count y downwards and 3D models upwards, so y is turned
  over. The entry's pins are the through-hole pads in number order.
- Materials: colour and transparency from VRML. Metal or not from the material's name (`PIN-`,
  `MET-` are metal), roughness from `shininess`.
- Writes a GLB (positions, normals, indices, one material per mesh) and the entry with its box and
  size. The output is the same bytes every time, so git diffs stay quiet.
- `check.sh` checks that `index.json` and `models/` match and that the licence file is there. It
  doesn't need KiCad to run.

**Which models (about 150), from `kicad-3dmodels`:** resistors lying flat (P7.62–15.24) and standing
(P2.54, P5.08); ceramic discs and dipped (P2.54, P5.00); electrolytics D5–D10 at their usual
pitches; film boxes; DO-35, DO-41 and DO-201AD diodes; 3 mm and 5 mm LEDs; TO-92 (inline and
wide); TO-220 standing and lying; DIP-8 to DIP-40, each plain and in a socket; pin headers and
female strips 1×2 to 1×20 and 2×N; 2-, 3- and 4-way screw terminals (5.08 and 3.5 mm); HC-49 and
HC-49/S crystals; trimmers and 9 mm pots; 6 mm and 12 mm buttons; Molex KK and JST XH 2–6 pin; a
DC barrel jack; USB-B and micro-USB through-hole; a buzzer; a small relay; slide switches.

**Models we make ourselves (added 2026-11-14, the owner: "we make the models that we are
missing and add to the library"):** parts no free library has are described as simple shapes
(boxes, cylinders) in `tools/our-models/<id>.json`, measured from their datasheets. The same
converter writes them as GLB into the same library, with `source.from` "Maker Forge" and licence
MIT. The first is the **MP1584 buck module** on the fume extractor (its board, the inductor, the
chip, the trimmer and the four pads), placed on the module's holes like any other model. Other
free libraries and datasheet sources come with Parts, once their licences are checked
(ROADMAP, "The Maker Forge Library").

**Budget:** 8 MB for all of them together, 250 KB for one. About 70 KB of `.wrl` comes out near
30 KB of GLB. A part over 250 KB (the DIP sockets' inner contacts) drops the faces that can't be
seen from outside, or stays out of the library and keeps Bench's own model.

## 2. Bench uses the models

**Matching a Bench part to a model** (`src/library-match.js`, data in, an `id` out, unit-tested):
1. A `model` field in the project or in Looks like (a library `id`) wins.
2. Otherwise by type and size: `res` by mount and hole span (the nearest library pitch); `cer` by
   style; `elec` by D and pitch; diodes by value (1N400x → DO-41, 1N540x → DO-201AD, 1N4148 →
   DO-35); `led` by 3 or 5 mm; `to92`; `to220` by facing; `dip` by pin count and holder (socket or
   not); headers by count, rows and female; crystals; pots; buttons; terminals.
3. No match (underside parts, links): Bench's own model, as today. The MP1584 module matches our
   own model (§1).

**Placing it:** pin 1 of the model goes on the part's pin-1 hole, turned so the model's pin 2 lies
towards the part's pin 2, and mirrored for parts under the board. Which of Bench's pin names is
KiCad's pin 1 follows KiCad's rules: on diodes and LEDs it's the cathode (K); on electrolytics it's
+; chips and headers go by number; three-leg parts in the order the project lists their pins. If the model's pins miss the
part's holes by more than 0.3 mm (a footprint that doesn't fit), Bench keeps its own model for that
part and says so in the 3D info.

**Painting what KiCad leaves plain:** the model's `body` material is tinted, and Bench's own
markings go on top: resistor colour bands (KiCad's resistor body is one colour); the
electrolytic's printed value and − stripe; the LED's colour; the chip's printed name. The bands
reuse 0.8's band rings, spaced along the model's body length from its box.

**Real or simple:** a **Parts: Real · Simple** switch in the 3D toolbar, remembered. Real is the
default. Simple is today's Bench models (lighter, and the fallback). The 3D info says how many
parts are real.

**Loading:** models load only when the 3D view opens, several at a time, cached by the browser.
Bench draws its simple model first and swaps each real one in as it arrives, so the view is never
empty. A thin bar says "Real parts 12 of 31".

**Looks like** gets **3D model:** the library models that fit the part's holes (the same pin count,
pins within 0.3 mm), each a button that swaps it in the 3D view, undoable. Also **Use my own
model…**: a `.glb` file from your computer, up to 5 MB, kept in this browser's own database
(IndexedDB, since it's too big for the settings store) and not in the progress file, which says
so. A `.glb` in the project folder (or repo) is offered too. With `dev.py`, so is a `.wrl`:
`dev.py` converts it with the same converter. A GLB from another program is in metres (glTF's
unit), so Bench scales it to millimetres, unless it's already bigger than 1 unit across. In that
case it was made in millimetres.

## 3. A better 3D view

1. **Truer light and materials:** an environment light made in code (three r128's
   `PMREMGenerator` over a simple sky-and-floor scene), so metal looks like metal and plastic like
   plastic. A soft shadow under each part, turned off by Less motion and on screens under 760 px
  wide.
2. **Board colours:** green, black, blue, red, white, purple, plus perfboard (brown, copper pads)
   and stripboard (copper strips) for Bench's own boards. In the 3D toolbar, kept per project.
3. **Your progress in 3D:** parts you've ticked show shiny solder joints underneath; the rest show
   bare legs.
4. **Which way round:** a toggle that floats a mark over each polarized part (the + leg, the
   cathode band, pin 1).
5. **The camera follows the guide:** opening a step turns the 3D view to that step's parts.
6. **Find in 3D:** a part found in Find lights up in 3D, and the camera flies to it.
7. **Saved views:** name the current view and keep up to nine, per project.
8. **Height check:** the tallest part on each side and its height, plus a "lid at __ mm" plane that
   shows what pokes through. Ready for the board in its box.
9. **Check my board in 3D:** its findings as red marks on the 3D board; clicking one opens the
   finding.
10. **Export:** GLB (the whole scene, real parts, through three's `GLTFExporter`) and OBJ (written
    by Bench, it's short), next to today's PNG and STL.
11. **Turntable video:** a 10-second turn of the board saved as WebM (the browser's own recorder;
    hidden where the browser can't record).
12. **Loading bar** for real parts.

## 4. Tests

- **Python** (`tests/python/test_kicad3d.py`, `tests/python/test_dev.py`):
  - the converter on a tiny `.wrl` fixture (two materials, a square and a triangle): ×2.54, the
    frame, a valid GLB, the same bytes twice;
  - `index.json` matches `models/`, the budget holds, and the licence file is there;
  - `dev.py` serves `library/` and converts a `.wrl` from the project folder, and nothing outside
    it.
- **Unit** (node): the model matching table, pin order and placing.
- **Browser:**
  - the fume extractor in Real and in Simple; a missing or broken model file keeps Bench's own;
  - the Looks like model picker, your own GLB (metres and millimetres), refused files;
  - board colours, progress joints, which way round, the camera following the guide, find in 3D;
  - saved views, the height check, Check my board marks, GLB and OBJ export, the turntable video;
  - the phone layout, and no page errors.
- **Existing tests:** the harness picks Simple parts unless a test asks for Real, the way it seeds
  the All tab today. So every existing 3D test keeps checking what it checks now.

## 5. Order of work

1. The converter, the models, the index and the licence file.
2. Bench uses the models: matching, placing, painting, Real or Simple, Looks like.
3. The 3D view upgrades (§3).
4. Manual, What's new, README, ROADMAP "done", and "As built" here.

## 6. Left to the builder

The final list of about 150 file names, the material-name rules, and where each new control sits.
Follow 0.8's patterns: the Menu, the panel tabs, and the 3D toolbar.

## Calls I made (the owner can change any)

- The library starts inside Bench (`src/library/`), ready to move.
- Models converted ahead of time to GLB in Python; no VRML parser in the browser.
- Real parts by default, with a Real/Simple switch; tests use Simple unless they ask.
- An 8 MB budget for all shipped models, 250 KB per model.
- Your own models: GLB in the browser (5 MB, this browser only), or a GLB in the project; `.wrl`
  too with `dev.py`; STEP later.
- Opening KiCad projects moved to 1.2 "More files" as an option, with GitHub able to run KiCad so
  nothing has to run on your computer (the owner: "just as the option for people importing their
  projects from there").
