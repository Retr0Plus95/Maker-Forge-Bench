# Bench 1.0.1 "At the bench": design

Status: **approved 2026-11-15** ("Yes, start batch 1"). Batch 1 of the 50 ideas in ROADMAP.md
("Waiting for a release: 50 more improvements"): ideas 1–10, 20 and 24. Built on `release-1.0.1`.

## 1. Why

The owner is building the fume extractor now. These twelve help at the bench itself: picking up
where you left off, knowing what's left, not mixing up parts, not making solder bridges (their board
may have one between the chip's pins 23 and 24), and carrying on when a part hasn't arrived yet.
Four of the five ideas picked to come first are here: 24 Bridge risk, 8 "I don't have this one yet",
20 Mix-up warning and 1 Welcome back. The fifth, L1 Fault finder, is batch 2.

## 2. Approaches

- **Batches, one release each (picked).** Twelve small and medium ideas go together; each batch gets
  its own branch, the full check and a review, and the owner chooses the merge.
- All 50 in one release: too big to review, and nothing reaches the bench for weeks.
- One release per idea: twelve merges for small things, with the same checks each time.

Inside the batch there's one sensible way for each idea, building on what Bench already has (the
guide, the part card, the build log, Check my board, the 3D marks, the settings dialog). §4 lists
where this design differs from the proposal and why.

## 3. What the builder gets

Everything you change is kept with your progress and can be undone (Ctrl+Z), like everything else.

1. **Welcome back.** Open a project whose last change was more than an hour ago, and the Guide tab
   starts with: "Welcome back. Last time (yesterday at 21:40): R6 soldered, step 7 of 10. Next: C4
   (100 nF), pin 1 at 3,17 · pin 2 at 4,17." **Carry on** opens that step and the part's card;
   **Close** hides it. With hole names set up on My board, the holes use them. Nothing ever done,
   nothing shown. Once per opening.
2. **Time left.** Under the steps' progress bar: "About 50 minutes left for the 12 parts still to
   solder, at your pace so far." The pace is your building time from the build log divided by the
   parts you've ticked; it shows from the third ticked part and five minutes of building. Rounded to
   5 minutes, or to 10 above an hour ("about 1 h 20 min").
3. **Iron reminder (off until you turn it on).** Settings → At the bench → "Ask if the iron is still
   on after 20 minutes without a tick". When on, after you've ticked something in this visit and then
   ticked nothing for 20 minutes: "Nothing ticked for 20 minutes. Is the iron still on?" with
   **Still working** (asks again in 20 minutes) and **It's off** (quiet until your next tick).
4. **Sort your parts first.** Menu → Sort your parts: each step in your order, its parts grouped by
   value with how many of each ("2 × 10k: R8, R9"), to lay out in a tray before you start. Waiting
   parts are under "Waiting for parts". **Print** prints just this list.
5. **Joints and solder on the shopping list.** Top of the shopping list: "About 180 solder joints:
   about 1.5 m of 0.8 mm solder, with some to spare." Joints: every hole with solder in it, counted
   once: each part leg, each link end, both holes of a bridge, and each bare bus at both ends and
   about every third hole (the fume extractor: 163 joints, about 2 m).
   Solder: about 8 mm of 0.8 mm wire per joint, plus a fifth to spare, rounded up to the next 0.5 m.
   Only with a board layout (the project's `board`).
6. **Tools for this step.** The open step says what to have at hand, from its parts and what it
   does: "Have ready: flush cutters, pliers." Legged parts → flush cutters; parts lying flat and
   bare buses → pliers; links → wire strippers and flush cutters; headers and sockets → tape or
   Blu-Tack to hold them; a checks step (its title says check, measure or power) → multimeter. The
   iron and solder go without saying.
7. **A soldering tip on each card.** One line from the part's type (or its library entry): "LEDs
   don't like heat: about 3 seconds a leg, then let it cool. The short leg is minus." Chips in a
   socket, electrolytics, diodes, transistors, headers, crystals, pots, switches, fuse holders and
   modules each get their own. The open step shows the tips of its kinds of parts once each.
8. **"I don't have this one yet."** A button on the card of a part not soldered yet. The part gets
   a "waiting" mark in the Parts list and on its card, leaves its step, and comes back in a
   **Waiting for parts** step at the end, each with the step it came from. A step whose parts are
   all waiting is left out until they come. Ticking a waiting part soldered takes it off the list;
   **It's here now** on its card does the same without ticking. The shopping list gets **Only
   what's missing**, and Copy and Save as spreadsheet follow it.
9. **A small buzz when you tick a part,** on phones that can (Android). Settings → At the bench →
   "Buzz when I tick a part", on by default. Unticking doesn't buzz.
10. **Undo by tap.** After a tick, for 6 seconds: "R6 soldered · Undo" at the bottom of the screen.
    Also for untick, Tick all, Done-next-step and "Tick all 5 × 10k". Undo is the same as Ctrl+Z,
    and only works while that tick is still the last change.
11. **Mix-up warning (idea 20).** A resistor whose colour bands differ from another value in the
    project by one band gets, on both cards: "Easy to mix up with R5 (1k): brown-black-orange against
    brown-black-red. Check the third band." The fume extractor has one: 10k and 1k.
12. **Bridge risk (idea 24).** Joints on different lines that sit one hole apart underneath, from
    different parts or a bare bus, get "watch for a bridge here":
    - in **Checks → Watch for bridges**, one line each: "J2 pin 1 (0 V) is next to the +12 V bus:
      keep the solder on its own pad." Two bare buses side by side are one line: "The +5 V bus (row
      18) and the 0 V bus (row 19) run side by side for 17 holes: keep a gap between them." Tapping
      a line opens its part;
    - in the **guide**, the open step lists the ones its parts (or its buses) make;
    - in **3D** (sheet 4's board), an amber mark under the board at each one, like Check my board's,
      behind a **Bridges** switch in the 3D bar (off at first: 26 marks would crowd the normal view,
      since marks show through the board). **Show them in 3D** in Checks turns it on and shows the
      underside. Tapping a mark opens its line in Checks.
    Legs of one part next to each other (a chip's pins) aren't listed: they're meant to be close,
    and the chip's tip says to use little solder. An underside part soldered onto a leg of its own
    line (C5 on the chip's pins 7 and 8) counts as that leg. The fume extractor gets about 26.
    **My board already has this** (Check my board's "solder these carefully, no bridge"), so it
    stays as it is.

Also, as basics found on the way: Bench's version number becomes 1.0.1 (it still said 0.9.0), and
"What's new" lists 1.0 and 1.0.1.

## 4. Where this differs from the proposal

- **No amber marks on the drawings themselves.** Bench knows where labels are on a sheet, not where
  each hole is, so it can't put a mark on a hole. The 3D underside and the Checks list show them.
- **Waiting parts are worked out, not copied:** a part counts as waiting when it's marked and not
  ticked. Ticking it takes it off the list, and undoing the tick puts it back, with no extra
  bookkeeping that undo could miss.
- **The buzz is a setting**, on by default, so it can be turned off.

## 5. Constraints

The usual ones hold: pages built with `el()` only, no innerHTML, Trusted Types `bench-svg` only;
every change through `history.do`; storage keys stay `bench.*`; `deploy/csp.txt` unchanged;
everything works on the website with nothing local. New progress fields are optional, so older
progress files still open, and are checked like the rest.
