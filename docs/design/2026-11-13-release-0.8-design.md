# Bench 0.8 "True to life" — design

Date: 2026-11-13. The owner asked: "make the 3d models even more accurate and the ui easier to
navigate and maybe more compacted with easy to follow steps and adjust". Their own build also
differs from the drawings: an L7805 in place of the MP1584 module, and the chip in two female
header strips instead of a DIP socket. So Bench should draw parts as they really look, let you say
what you actually fitted, and lead you through the build one step at a time.

Built in two halves: the 3D and "your parts" first (they don't touch the screens 0.7 is
changing), then the compact screens and the guide, on top of 0.7.

## 1. 3D models true to the datasheet

- **A new shape: `lathe`**, a profile spun round an axis, standing up or lying between two points.
  It gives round parts their real outline:
  - **Resistors** (Yageo CFR-25): end caps 2.5 mm across and 1.4 mm long, a 2.2 mm waist, 6.3 mm
    overall. The bands sit as on a real part: three at one cap, the tolerance band alone at the
    other.
  - **Electrolytics** (Nichicon UVR / Panasonic): the rubber bung, the bead groove near the bottom,
    the sleeve rolled over the top edge, the aluminium top with its vent. The value is printed on
    the sleeve, away from the − stripe.
  - **LEDs** (Kingbright L-7104): the flange with its flat on the cathode side, the dome in one.
  - **DO-201AD diodes**: rounded ends, the silver band at the cathode.
  - **Ceramic discs**: a rounded edge and the coating running down the legs.
- **The chip (Microchip PDIP-28):**
  - the body has its mould line: 7.24 mm wide below it, 6.6 mm above;
  - the notch is cut into the end and pin 1's dimple is beside it;
  - each leg is the real flat lead: a 1.52 mm shoulder, then a 0.46 mm tip.
- **What the chip sits in**, one of three:
  - a DIP socket: an open frame with a contact opening at each pin;
  - two female header strips (8.5 mm tall), as on the owner's board;
  - soldered straight in.
- **New parts:**
  - TO-220 regulators (ST L7805 drawing): a 10.2 × 9.2 × 4.5 mm body, a 1.27 mm tab with its
    3.8 mm hole, three legs 2.54 mm apart;
  - female header strips.
- **Smaller fixes:**
  - the crystal gets its base flange;
  - header pins get pointed tips.
- Every size keeps its source next to it in `SIZES`, and the unit tests check the important ones.

## 2. Your parts: adjust a part to what you fitted

On every board part's card, **Looks like** offers what that kind of part can be. The choice is
kept with your progress, undoable, and saved in progress files.

- **The chip:** in a DIP socket, in two header strips, or soldered in.
- **Resistors:** lying or standing; a beige (carbon) or blue (metal film) body.
- **Electrolytics:** diameter (5, 6.3, 8, 10 mm) and height.
- **Ceramics:** an orange disc or a blue dipped one.
- **LEDs:** red, green, yellow, blue or white.
- **Another part in its place** (the library's alternatives):
  - the MP1584 module can be an **L7805** (TO-220);
  - its pins map across: IN from IN+, GND from IN− and OUT−, OUT from OUT+;
  - the card, the 3D view, the wiring list, Check my board, the continuity test and Your board
    all follow;
  - the 7805's card warns it can't power the USB port.
- **Pure and tested:** `src/looks.js` cleans what's saved and turns the project's board into the
  board as you built it.

## 3. Easy to follow (on top of 0.7)

- **The panel in tabs:**
  - one short tab row: **Guide · Board · Wiring · Checks · Parts**, each with its count;
  - one tab shows at a time, instead of a long stack of sections;
  - the part card stays on top of whichever tab is open.
- **The guide:** one step at a time, in big plain words:
  - step N of M and a progress bar;
  - what to do and why;
  - each part with where it goes (hole names when they're on) and a tick;
  - Show in 3D, and a big **Done, next step**.
- **Adjust the steps:**
  - move a step up or down, skip one, or add your own step (a title and a line of text);
  - kept with your progress, undoable;
  - **Back to the project's steps** puts them back.
- **A compact header on wide screens too:** the everyday tools stay, the rest go in 0.7's Menu.
- **Easy reading** gets a **Compact** density next to the text size.

## 4. Order

The 3D shapes, then your parts, then (once 0.7 is merged) the tabs, the guide, the step changes
and the header. Tests first; push as each lands.
