# Bench 1.0.2 "Fault finder": design

Status: **my calls, built on the owner's "and keep going" (2026-11-15)** after they merged 1.0.1.
This is batch 2 of the 50 ideas in ROADMAP.md: L1 Fault finder by symptom. Built on `release-1.0.2`;
the owner reads this and reviews the branch before they choose the merge.

## 1. Why

The owner's board has open faults right now: the fans always run at 100 %, the LCD says "!BOTH FANS
STOP" (R12 and R13 aren't fitted), and pins 23 and 24 may be bridged. Bench already knows every
connection on the board, every part's job (its `role` in the project file) and which way round each
part goes. So it can walk you from what you see ("the fans always run at full speed") to the meter
checks that find the cause, show where each probe goes, and hand everything you measured to Claude
when the checks run out.

## 2. Approaches

- **From the project's connections and the parts' roles (picked).** The symptoms come from what's on
  the board: the power rails, each chip, each connector (by what its role says it goes to: a fan, a
  display, a light, a knob, a button) and LEDs, knobs and buttons on the board itself. The checks
  follow the connections from that thing to the chip and the rails. Works for any project with a
  `board` section, with nothing written by hand.
- Written per project, a `faults` list in `forge-project.json`: the most exact, but no project has
  one and every author would have to write it. It can come later on top of this.
- Claude only: needs a key or Ollama, guesses what the board already knows, and can't show where to
  measure. Here Claude is the last step instead.

## 3. What the builder gets

A new section in the Checks tab, **Find a fault**, between the power-up checks and the continuity
test. Also in the Ctrl+K palette ("Find a fault").

1. **Pick what's wrong.** On the fume extractor:
   - Nothing happens when I switch it on
   - Something gets hot, smokes or smells
   - U2 (ATmega328P-PU) doesn't seem to run
   - Fan A (J2) doesn't spin · Fan A (J2) always runs at full speed (and the same for Fan B, J3)
   - The LCD (J4) stays blank
   - The knob (J5 pin 2) does nothing · LED A (J5 pin 4) doesn't light · LED B (J5 pin 5) doesn't light
   - The button (J6) does nothing

   Each shows how far you got ("4 of 15 checked · 1 problem"). A project without connections says
   the fault finder needs them, and offers Ask Claude in your own words instead.
2. **One check at a time.** Each check says what to do, where the probes go (part and pin, with the
   hole's name when hole names are set up on My board), what to expect, and why, from the part's role
   ("R6 holds Q1 off, so the fans run at 100 % if the controller is dead or in reset"). Four kinds:
   - **Look**: "Is R12 (4.7k) fitted, in its own holes?" → Looks right / Found a problem.
   - **Beep** (power off, meter on beep): "R12 pin 2 and U2 pin 4 should beep." → Beeped / No beep.
     The short checks are the other way: "+5 V and 0 V must not beep."
   - **Volts** (power on, black probe on 0 V): type what the meter shows. Where the board says what it
     should be (a rail, or a line held up by a resistor to a rail with nothing else on it), Bench
     judges it: ±10 %. Otherwise it's kept as a reading for Claude.
   - **Watch**: "Turn the knob fully one way, then the other: does the reading go from about 0 V up to
     about +5 V?" → Yes / No. The button's is "It beeps only while you hold the button down."

   Power-off checks come first, then "Now switch on" and the power-on ones. "Something gets hot" is
   power-off only and starts with "Switch off and unplug it now."
   A wrong answer says what it means and what to fix ("No connection between R12 pin 2 and U2 pin 4:
   a joint is open or a part is missing. Reflow both joints."), with **Fixed it, check again** and
   **Carry on**. **Back** goes to the check before; **Skip** leaves one for later.
   **Show where** selects the part with that pin picked in its card's pin view, rings it on the
   sheet, and with 3D open puts a red + and a black − mark over the two probe holes.
3. **What the checks found.** Always under the checks: the problems found, each with its fix, and
   your readings. **Ask Claude** sends the symptom, every check and answer, the readings and the
   roles of the parts involved to Claude or Ollama (when set up in Easy reading) and shows the answer.
   Without that, **Copy for Claude** copies the same text and opens claude.ai, as the card does.
4. **Kept and undoable.** Answers are kept with your progress per symptom (progress field `faults`,
   in the progress file too), each one a change you can undo, named in the build log ("U2 pin 7 to
   0 V: 4.98 V"). **Start this one again** clears one symptom's answers.

## 4. How the checks are made (for the builder)

- **Rails**: nets whose `netLabels` read as volts ("+12 V", "+5 V", "0 V"; a net called GND is 0 V).
- **The way in**: the connector with a pin on the highest rail and one on 0 V whose role says power,
  supply, jack or battery; failing that, the first such connector. Its pins are where the probes go
  for "+12 V" and "0 V" unless the thing being checked has its own 0 V pin.
- **What's on the path**: from the thing's pins (not its rail pins), follow each net through
  resistors, transistors, diodes, LEDs, crystals, knobs and switches, up to 4 parts deep. Stop at
  chips, connectors and rails. Capacitors are looked at but not gone through.
- **Path checks**, in walking order: a look at each part (fitted, its value; the way round from its
  `orientation` when it's polarized), a beep between each pair of neighbouring members of each net
  (as the continuity test does), then, with the power on, the volts on each net.
- **By symptom**:
  - nothing happens: the off-board fuse and switch (fuse: a beep across it), no short between each
    pair of rails, the way round of each polarized part on a rail, then the way in's volts and each
    rail's volts;
  - gets hot: no short between rails, the polarized parts, each module's IN and OUT, the rails that
    run side by side (the 1.0.1 bridge risks of kind `buses`);
  - a chip: its way round and seating, no short between neighbouring pins where one is a rail, each
    0 V pin beeps to the way in's 0 V, each rail pin's volts, each pulled-up pin's volts, the crystal
    and its capacitors;
  - a fan that doesn't spin: the volts between its rail pins, each rail pin beeps to the way in, the
    plug the right way round, then its path checks;
  - a fan at full speed: its signal pins' path checks;
  - a display: the volts between its rail pins, its path checks, the contrast screw, the wires in the
    order of the display module's labels;
  - a light: the LED's way round (long leg +), its path checks;
  - a knob: the watch check, the outer legs on the rail and 0 V, its path checks;
  - a button: the press-and-beep check, its path checks.
- Check ids come from what they check (`look:R12`, `beep:R12:2|U2:4`, `volts:TACHA`,
  `short:P5V|GND`), so answers stay with the right check after a reload.

## 5. Not in this release

- A `faults` list written in the project file.
- Checks that know the firmware (Bench doesn't read the code).
- The 1.4 measurement log with expected values from the project file.

## 6. Where this differs from the roadmap's line

- "Lighting where to measure" is the part's ring on the sheet, the pin in the card's pin view and the
  probe marks in 3D. The drawings get no hole marks, for the same reason as 1.0.1: Bench knows where
  the labels are on a sheet, not where each hole is.
- "Claude helps when the checks run out" works without a key too: Copy for Claude.
